---
title: 'Disrupt Yourself or Be Disrupted'
date: '2026-10-13'
description: 'As AI makes software easier to copy, the tech companies that keep growing will be the ones that iterate on proprietary customer data.'
---

I believe the tech landscape has changed dramatically behind the scenes over the past few years.

Tech companies without an established pipeline for collecting constant, labelled user data will
see their growth flatten faster than most people expect. The companies that keep growing will be
the ones that constantly iterate on their customer data effectively. While technology is here to
stay, and I believe it will remain among the highest growth sectors, only the companies that keep
disrupting themselves will continue growing the way tech companies have over the past few decades.

## Surviving vs Flourishing

I'm not saying these companies will die. At the end of the day, software is a marginal-cost
business. Once the product is built, serving another customer costs them close to nothing, so most
of them will probably stay profitable for a long time.

What I'm saying is that their revenue growth may be limited. We're in an era where data and compute
is everywhere, and if you don't have a way to iterate on your product with that, you're basically
relying on your team's guesses about what users want. Meanwhile, the companies constantly iterating
on their product with proprietary customer data get to see what actually works. Over time, I think
those companies will take more and more market share from the incumbents who fail to disrupt
themselves. More importantly, (I believe) spotting these companies is paramount to making
asymmetrical returns.

## Spotify

One company I believe has been disrupting itself well is Spotify ($SPOT).

They've been collecting listener data for years and analyzing it to better understand what keeps
users listening, what types of music users stay around for, when they skip, and so on. Since they
collect labelled data, they can iterate on their product with confidence, knowing that each change
they make comes back with specific metrics telling them what worked and what didn't.

Spotify is the biggest music streaming platform in the world, which makes them the largest generator
of listener data in the world. This inevitably makes Spotify end up being the largest owner and
analyzer of listener data too.

You could argue YouTube sees more listening than Spotify does, but YouTube can't easily tell whether
someone is actually listening to a song or just has a video playing in the background. On Spotify,
paid users are there primarily to listen, so every play, skip, and save is a cleaner signal.

It's similar to compound interest. Every improvement keeps people listening longer, which gives
Spotify more data, which feeds the next improvement. A generic streaming app launching today starts
that loop from zero, and the gap between it and Spotify keeps widening the more data Spotify
collects.

Spotify also has a network effect on top of its data. Every listener's behavior improves
recommendations for listeners with similar taste, so the product gets better for everyone as more
people join. Shared playlists and Wrapped make the app more fun, and artists and podcasters put
their work on Spotify first because that's where the listeners are.

While replicating a music streaming app isn't hard anymore, what they can't replicate is Spotify's
database of listener data, or the pipeline they've built for collecting, analyzing, and iterating on
it.

Spotify's stock closed 2022 at about $79, right as generative AI started taking off, and trades
around $529 today. That's up roughly 570%, or about 65% a year compounded.

## Adobe

One company I believe will face limited growth is Adobe ($ADBE) (take with a grain of salt).

Adobe collects user data, but the improvements they implement tend to be very visible
functionality, such as new tools or AI features integrated into their product. As technology
progresses, these features get easier and easier to copy.

Compare that to Spotify, where the listener algorithm sits on the backend. Most of Adobe's software
services are visible to the user, so mirroring a new Adobe update (REA: Reverse Engineer Anything)
isn't nearly as difficult as mirroring an algorithm Spotify has been building and improving over
multiple years.

By metrics, Adobe's revenue hasn't stalled. Since 2022, when generative AI started becoming popular,
it has grown about 10.5% a year on average.

## Software is getting commoditized

I like to think of it like the manufacturing of apparel. Once the world figured out how to
manufacture clothes cheaply, pretty much anyone could make a shirt (shoutout Alibaba), competing
mostly on price and branding.

I think the same thing is starting to happen to software. With how fast AI capabilities are
growing, the world is increasingly gaining access to the manufacturing of software. When almost
anyone can build an app, software on its own starts to become a commodity.

This is already showing up in small ways. Earlier this year, Adobe said its stock image business
shrank faster than expected as customers switched to AI-generated images. People used to pay to
license a photo, now they just generate one. I think that's a preview of what happens to software
that can be reproduced.

The apparel brands that escaped price competition did it through brand. In software, I think the
equivalent is a product that keeps improving in ways competitors can't see or copy, and that comes
from proprietary data.

Code is getting easier to produce every year, but years of proprietary customer data and a pipeline
that iterates on it isn't something you can easily generate.

## What this means for investing

In the next few years, I think one of the highest EV things you can do as an investor is figure out
which tech companies are able to iterate effectively with proprietary customer data, and which
can't. The former will keep growing. The latter will slowly falter.

When I look at a tech company now, I ask three questions:

- Does it own and generate proprietary data?
- Does it have a pipeline that turns that data into product improvements?
- Are these improvements easily copied?

A lot of people right now are focused on finding the "next bottleneck" of the AI trade, whether
that's chips, data centers, or power.

Instead, I think one of the bigger opportunities is in companies that benefit from AI without having
to bet on who wins the AI race. Whichever chipmaker or model comes out on top, a company with years
of proprietary data gets to use it, and that data is something no AI lab can sell to its
competitors.
