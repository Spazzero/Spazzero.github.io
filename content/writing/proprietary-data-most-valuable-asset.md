I believe the companies that win the next ten years won’t be the ones with the
best software. The companies that utilise their proprietary data the best will
be the incumbents of the future. By proprietary data, I refer to information a
company collects that no competitor can buy or copy on the open market.

## The Software Model and its limits

Since the dot-com boom around the turn of the century, tech stocks have gone on
a run I would call an absolute dominance, lasting well into the 2020s.

A big reason for that run is what I call the Software Model. Building software
is expensive at the start, but once it's built, selling it to one more customer
costs almost nothing. This near-zero marginal cost gives software companies high
operating leverage, which other industries can't match. Once a company covers
its fixed costs, most of each extra dollar of revenue turns into profit and free
cash flow.

I don’t believe this will be the case for companies only offering good software
in the coming decade.

Some tech giants will remain safe for now. $AAPL and $META, for example, are
deeply rooted in our daily lives. Apple’s ecosystem connects our phone, laptop,
cloud storage, and work. The longer users stay, the more deeply entrenched they
become in the ecosystem. Meta benefits from its network effect. The more people
you know on the platform, the more valuable the platform becomes for you.

I don’t see that kind of advantage for less sticky platforms such as $ADBE. The
majority of Adobe’s revenue still comes from Creative Cloud, their subscription
bundle of Photoshop, Illustrator, etc.

![Adobe annual revenue by segment, 2023 to 2025. Digital Media is the largest segment every year, reaching 17.65B USD in 2025, against 5.86B USD for Digital Experience and 256M USD for Publishing and Advertising.](/images/writing/proprietary-data/adobe-revenue-by-segment.png)

While these are incredibly useful tools and are insanely profitable for the
company, the fact of the matter is that the market is forward-looking. A stock
price reflects what investors expect a company to earn in the future, and not
what they earn today. If growth stagnates while the moat of the Software Model
weakens, companies that rely on B2B or B2C without offering additional value to
the end customer will eventually lose those customers to hungrier competitors.

## Data is only as good as the processing

So if software on its own isn’t enough, what is? The company that made the
importance of proprietary data clear to me is Palantir, one of the
fastest-growing tech companies of recent years.

Companies have been collecting data for decades. The trouble was that the value
coming out of it was limited. Corporate data is often fragmented, sitting in
separate systems and formats, and analysing them together wasn’t worth the
hassle.

Palantir’s approach is to build a “digital twin” of a business, or an ontology.
The idea is to represent every portion of the company in data, from the supply
chain to the staff and customers. Upon connecting this data chain, companies
would be able to streamline how they operate, test changes on their digital twin
before production, and optimise their business in ways traditional methods
can’t.

I like to think of it the same way I’d think about building a simple predictive
model. If the data you process and train your model with is shit, the model
stays shit. No amount of clever modelling fixes bad inputs. Anyone who has done
machine learning knows that data processing is about 80% of the work, while the
actual modelling (the fun part) is a small slice at the end.

I see Palantir as the data processor for companies. It takes the messy data its
clients have been sitting on and turns it into something they can actually use.

## Why I’m watching Duolingo

With how fast AI is advancing, and new models coming out every week, good
software is getting easier to build and copy. If anyone can access a powerful
model, it's fair to ask what any single company still has that others don't.

I’m in the camp that believes the answer is data. Whichever company can collect
and process the highest quality proprietary data will be the winner of the next
decade.

Duolingo is an example of a company I think the market is mispricing. Its stock
price has dropped more than 80% in under a year, from $544.93 in May 2025 to
$87.89 in April 2026.

![Weekly chart of Duolingo (NASDAQ: DUOL) from late 2022 to October 2026. The price peaks near $545 in May 2025, falls about 84% to a low under $90 in April 2026, then recovers to around $147.](/images/writing/proprietary-data/duolingo-share-price.png)

While mainstream media has been pricing Duolingo like a dead bird, the dead bird
is the largest generator of learner data in the world. Every lesson, mistake,
and streak is incremental data on how people learn languages, and no other
company collects labelled learner data at a similar scale.

![Duolingo’s summary of financial and key operating metrics, Q2 2025 against Q2 2026. Daily active users grew 23% from 47.7M to 58.7M and revenue grew 18% to $298.5M, while net income fell 26% to $33.2M and adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 31.2% to 25.9%.](/images/writing/proprietary-data/duolingo-q2-2026-metrics.png)

Duolingo keeps growing its daily active users (DAUs), from 47.7M in Q2 2025 to
58.7M in Q2 2026. More active users mean more data, and more data means a better
product and a better understanding of how to keep users coming back.

Profits have slowed this year because Duolingo chose to spend on user growth,
and I think the market is reading that as a decline. I believe the bird has room
to thrive in the coming decade.
